Earning ₹2.11 Lakh, Owing ₹17 Lakh: A Way Out of the Loan Trap

13 November 2026 · AdRaw App Studio

A real story from r/IndiaFinance, a month-by-month way out, and why tracking every rupee matters more than earning more.

The post that stopped us scrolling

A few days ago someone on r/IndiaFinance asked for help. They take home ₹2,11,252 a month, a salary most Indians would envy. They still can't save a single rupee.

The reason: ₹17.17 lakh of debt spread across a credit card, two bank loans and five instant-loan apps. It started with a family emergency. They needed money fast and took whatever was approved. They aren't making excuses. They want a plan, they're ready to cut back, and they don't want another loan.

They have already done two smart things. They moved to a cheaper flat, which cut rent and household help from about ₹36,000 to ₹25,000. They also stopped using the credit card two or three months ago.

This story is more common than people admit. So let's work through it properly.

The salary is ₹2,11,252, so they are still about ₹6,000 short every month. Before the move the gap was around ₹17,000. That short gap is the danger. It gets covered by the card, by a new app loan, or by a late payment, and the hole gets deeper.

One number matters most here. At these rates the interest alone is roughly ₹40,000–45,000 a month. A big part of every EMI never touches the principal.

The good news hiding in the numbers

This looks hopeless at first. It isn't. The income is strong, and the two cheapest loans are almost finished.

  • HDFC (₹1.15 lakh at ₹25,698 a month) should close in about 5 months.
  • ICICI (₹1.49 lakh at ₹23,769 a month) should close in about 6–7 months.

When those two close, about ₹49,500 a month frees up. That's the turning point. What matters is where that money goes next.

The plan: four phases

Phase 1: Stop the bleeding (now)

  1. Keep the card frozen. Don't just stop using it. Remove it from Amazon, Swiggy, Google Pay and every saved wallet.
  2. Close the ₹6,000 gap this month. Trim miscellaneous spending, cut back on the cook for a few months, or cancel subscriptions. Spending must be lower than income, even if only by ₹2,000.
  3. Never miss an EMI. One missed payment adds a late fee, extra interest and a dent in your CIBIL score. If a payment is in danger, call the lender before the due date.
  4. Keep a small buffer of ₹10,000–15,000. Without it, the next surprise expense goes straight back onto the card.

Phase 2: Make the card cheaper (month 1)

Revolving credit card debt is usually the most expensive money in this whole picture. Call the card's bank and ask to convert the outstanding amount into an EMI plan. You're not borrowing anything new. You're repaying the same debt at a much lower rate. Ask about the processing fee and GST before you agree.

Phase 3: Avalanche (months 1–7)

Pay every EMI on time. Put every extra rupee on the most expensive debt. That's usually the card, then FlexSalary, then the other app loans. As each debt closes, its whole payment moves to the next one. That's the debt avalanche.

If you need quick wins to stay motivated, the snowball method also works: clear Zype (₹40,000) first just to have one fewer app. Avalanche saves the most money. Snowball keeps more people going. Either one beats having no plan.

Before you prepay any app loan, check its KFS for foreclosure charges. Usually it's still worth it.

Phase 4: Roll the freed EMIs forward (months 5–12)

When HDFC and ICICI close, don't let your lifestyle grow into that ₹49,500. Send all of it to the next debt on the list. Do the same with increments, bonuses, tax refunds and any side income.

What not to do

  • Don't take a new loan to pay an old EMI. That's how 2 apps became 7.
  • Be careful with "debt settlement" agencies. A settled account shows up on your CIBIL report for years and makes future home or car loans much harder.
  • Don't ignore recovery calls. Answer them, explain your repayment plan, and keep a record. Harassment by digital lenders can be reported to the lender's grievance officer and then to the RBI Ombudsman.
  • Don't touch the emergency buffer for wants. It's there for emergencies only.
  • Don't carry this alone. Money stress is real. Tell one person you trust what's going on.

The real problem: money that leaves without being noticed

Here's the uncomfortable truth in this post. The problem wasn't the income. It was not being able to see the money. Five app loans, a card, two bank loans, all charging on different dates and sending alerts that get swiped away. At ₹2.11 lakh a month you can lose track of ₹10,000–20,000 without feeling it, and that's exactly the gap that fills up the credit card.

No plan survives if you only find out at month-end that you overspent. You need to see it as it happens.
